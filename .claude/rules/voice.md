# Voice

A professor who respects the reader's time.

- Short sentences. Active voice. Concrete nouns and verbs. Second person.
- Order inside a section: claim → mechanism → example → boundary. Concrete before abstract. Then stop. A second claim is a second section.
- The mechanism sentence answers *why* in terms of what the reader, system, or process does. A name or a citation is not a mechanism. A rule without its mechanism in the next sentence is an assertion: give the why or cut the rule.
- Show every example you mention, in full. Never describe a sentence or case you do not quote.
- Teach a rule on a contrast pair: two versions that differ by one move, and what each does to the reader.
- The boundary is a shown case where the rule fails or misleads.
- Define a term once, in bold, before its first use. Use that word every time after.
- When sources offer two analyses, give the one the writer needs. Drop the other.
- Headings and titles state a claim or name a thing. Never "Introduction" or "Overview."
- Banned: "interestingly," "note that," "it's worth," "let's," "in other words," "simply," em-dashes, rhetorical questions outside `:::predict`, previews, closing recaps, stacked hedges, anecdotes or history unless they are the point.
- 150–300 words of prose per section; 350 is the ceiling. Over it means two ideas: split, or cut the paragraph that only restates.
- Density by the objective's `level` in `learner.md`:
  - novice: connectives spelled out, referents restated, worked example before the rule.
  - familiar: rule, then contrast pair; name the move they already make by instinct.
  - proficient: problem before example; one gap per section left open as a `:::predict` "why" question.
