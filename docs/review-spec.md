# Spec: Daily review

A daily (or whenever) test of what you know across every course. Anki's loop — a queue of due items, graded, rescheduled — with two differences: the items are the course's own questions, graded by the course's own grader, and the system already knows what you got wrong and why.

## What exists

Every question you answer in a lesson, test or review becomes an FSRS card (`cards` table; `library/state/cards/<course>/<question>.json`). Rating comes from result and confidence: correct + Sure → Easy, correct → Good, partial → Hard, wrong → Again. "Learned" is three spaced correct recalls in the Review state. The per-course **Review** tab serves due cards one at a time. There is no cross-course view, no daily rhythm, no limit on a session, no variants (you see the same wording every time), and nothing to tell you how you are doing.

## Goals

1. One place to review everything: `/review` at the top level, and a "Due today: 14" line on the courses page.
2. A daily session that takes 5–15 minutes and ends. Not an infinite queue.
3. Recall of ideas, not of wording: after the first review, a card can be asked as a variant.
4. The grader's feedback and the learner profile keep doing their job inside review.
5. Ad hoc modes for when you want more: a course, a module, weak spots, a timed burst.

## Non-goals

Flashcard authoring by hand; importing Anki decks; leaderboards; mobile notifications (local app; the courses page is the reminder).

## Cards

**Unit.** One card per quiz `id`, as now: section checks, module tests, warm-up reviews, drill-down questions, baseline items. The source doc and objective travel with it.

**Lifecycle.** `new` (answered once in a lesson, never reviewed) → `learning` → `review` → `learned` (streak ≥ 3 in Review). `suspended` by the learner (× on a card: "stop asking this"). `leech`: six lapses → suspended automatically with a note in the profile's `errors`; the next module generation picks it up as a `carry` item. A card whose source doc no longer contains its id is `orphaned` and hidden.

**Variants.** The second time a card comes up, and every time after, the question may be a **variant**: same objective, same idea units, a different case — generated by a `review-variant` skill (light lane) and stored beside the card (`variants: [{prompt, rubric|options+answer+explanation, createdAt}]`, up to three, rotated). Variants come from the learner's `domains`, rotating through them. MCQ variants keep the same misconception distractors in new clothes. The original wording returns one time in four so recognition is still checked.

**Difficulty signal to the learner.** Each card shows its interval after grading ("next in 3 days"), as Anki does; it is the cheapest honest feedback on how well something is held.

## The daily session

**Composition.** Cards due now, across all courses, up to a cap (default 20, setting). Interleaved: never two cards from the same module in a row when another module is available. If fewer than 8 are due, top up with the oldest `new` cards (answered once, never reviewed) so a day with nothing due still exercises recent material. Nothing from a module you have not finished reading past its section, so review never spoils a lesson.

**Order.** Due-date order within the interleave, except: cards whose last result was wrong-and-Sure come first (hypercorrection: the error is fresh and the learner's confidence in it is the thing to break).

**Flow.** One card at a time. Prompt → answer → confidence → Check → feedback (grader note for short; explanation for MCQ) → next. Short answers grade asynchronously; the session does not wait: the next card opens while the previous grades, and the result slides in. Keyboard: 1/2/3 for confidence, Enter to check, Enter again for next.

**Ending.** The session ends when the queue is empty: a summary line ("14 reviewed · 10 right · 2 sure-and-wrong · next due tomorrow") and the door to ad hoc. No "review ahead" button on the daily page; that is what ad hoc is for.

**Daily rhythm.** Due dates are per card; "today" is simply what is due by now. The courses page shows the count and a one-line streak ("reviewed 6 days running"); the streak counts days with at least one review and is informational, not a badge.

## Ad hoc modes

From `/review`, after the daily queue or any time:

- **Course** or **module**: every card from it that is not suspended, due or not, in interleaved order; grading and scheduling as normal (reviewing early is allowed; FSRS handles it).
- **Weak spots**: cards with a lapse in the last 30 days, or whose objective is `partial`/`unmet` in an `understanding.md`, or tagged with a pattern in `learner.md` `errors`. Capped at 20.
- **Burst**: 10 cards, random across courses, timed softly (a clock, no cutoff).
- **Cram before a wrap-up**: the module's section cards, variants preferred, offered from the wrap-up page before "Start discussion."

## Grading and feedback

As in lessons: MCQ locally, short answers through `/grade` with the rubric's idea units and the learner's `errors`. Confidence is required before Check. Wrong-and-Sure shows the hypercorrection line. A review of a variant records against the original card's id (one schedule per idea). Each review appends to the card's log with `variant: n|null` so the profile can tell "knows the idea" from "knows the wording."

## Scheduling

FSRS as now, `request_retention: 0.9`. Add: a per-course or global **retention setting** (0.85–0.95) for learners who want fewer reviews; a nightly **optimizer** run once there are ~400 reviews (`fsrs-rs-nodejs` or ts-fsrs's `reschedule`) to fit the weights to this learner; "learned" stays at three spaced recalls. Suspended and orphaned cards never schedule.

## What the profile learns from review

`wrap-module` already reads cards. Add to `learner.md` from review data: `calibration` running counts (sure-and-wrong, guess-and-right) across all courses; `retention` per course (share of reviews rated Good or better in the last 30 days); `leeches` (card ids), which feed `carry`. `generate-module` reads `retention` to decide how much warm-up review to open with.

## Data

```
cards            + state (new|learning|review|learned|suspended|orphaned), lapses, variants JSON, last_variant
review_sessions  id, started_at, ended_at, kind (daily|course|module|weak|burst|cram), scope, reviewed, correct, sure_wrong
settings         key, value   (daily cap, retention target)
```

All under `library/state/` like everything else; sessions are small append-only files, settings one file.

## Prompts

- `review-variant <slug> <doc> <question-id>`: read the question and its rubric, the section it came from, and `learner.md`; write one variant with the same idea units, a case from a domain the original did not use, and for MCQ the same misconceptions as distractors. Structured output (`--json-schema`), no file writes. Light lane, ~10 s.
- `grade`: unchanged; takes the variant's rubric when a variant was asked.

## UI

- Courses page: "Due today: 14 · 6-day streak" line above the course list, linking to `/review`.
- `/review`: the daily queue; on empty, the ad hoc menu and the last session's summary. Card shows course · module · "variant" badge when applicable; after grading, the interval.
- Card actions: suspend (×), "show the section" (link), "flag wrong grade" (writes a note the next `grade` run sees).
- Keyboard throughout.

## Build order

1. Cross-course `/review` with daily cap, interleave, top-up from `new`, session summary and streak. Mostly a query change plus a page. *(half a day)*
2. Card lifecycle: suspend, leech → profile, orphan hiding. *(half a day)*
3. Variants: skill, storage, rotation, grading against the variant rubric. *(one day)*
4. Ad hoc modes and cram from the wrap-up page. *(half a day)*
5. Retention setting, optimizer, profile fields. *(one day, after there is enough data)*

## Open questions

- Should baseline questions become cards? They are diagnostic, not taught; proposal: no, unless the learner later answers them correctly in a module.
- Daily cap as a hard stop or a soft one with "10 more"? Proposal: hard on the daily page, unlimited in ad hoc.
- Variants for MCQ: regenerate options or keep options and change only the stem? Proposal: change the case in the stem, keep the misconceptions, let wording vary.
